Networking Fundamentals – Questions & Answers

1. What is a computer network?
A computer network is a group of computers and devices connected together to share data, resources, and services such as files, printers, and internet access.
2. What is a network protocol?
A network protocol is a set of rules that devices follow to communicate and exchange data over a network. Examples include HTTP, FTP, TCP, IP, and DNS.
3. What is an IP address?
An IP address is a unique logical address assigned to a device on a network. It helps identify the device and allows data to be delivered to the correct destination.
4. What is the difference between LAN and WAN?
LAN (Local Area Network): Covers a small area such as a home, school, or office. WAN (Wide Area Network): Covers a large geographical area, such as cities, countries, or the entire world. The Internet is the largest example of a WAN.
5. What is a MAC address?
A MAC address is a unique hardware address associated with a network interface. It is mainly used for communication within a local network.
6. What is a router?
A router is a networking device that connects different networks and forwards data packets toward their destination. A home Wi-Fi router, for example, connects your local devices to the internet.
7. What is a switch?
A switch is a networking device used to connect multiple devices within a local network. It forwards data to the appropriate device using MAC addresses.
8. What is DNS?
DNS (Domain Name System) converts easy-to-remember domain names, such as example.com, into IP addresses that computers use to locate servers.
9. What is the OSI model?
The OSI (Open Systems Interconnection) model is a seven-layer conceptual model used to understand how data travels through a network. Its layers are Application, Presentation, Session, Transport, Network, Data Link, and Physical.
10. What is the difference between TCP and UDP?
TCP (Transmission Control Protocol): Reliable and connection-oriented. It checks that data is delivered correctly and in order. UDP (User Datagram Protocol): Faster and connectionless, but it does not guarantee delivery or ordering.
11. What are the three types of networks based on geographical area?
LAN (Local Area Network), MAN (Metropolitan Area Network), and WAN (Wide Area Network).
12. Describe Star Topology and its main drawback?
All nodes are connected to a central host node, which controls communication between them. Advantages: low cost, easy to add nodes, failure of one node doesn't affect others. Drawback: if the host/central node fails, the entire network fails.
13. What is Transmission Media? Name its two categories?
The physical medium through which data signals travel between devices. Two categories: Guided Media (wired, e.g., cables) and Unguided Media (wireless, e.g., radio waves, microwaves, infrared).
14. What is a Modem? Expand the term?
Modem = Modulator + Demodulator. It's a device that converts digital signals (from a computer) into analog signals (for transmission over telephone lines) and vice versa.
15. Name and explain the three modes of data transmission?
Simplex (data flows in only one direction, e.g., keyboard-to-computer), Half Duplex (data flows both ways but only one direction at a time, e.g., walkie-talkie/telephone-style), and Full Duplex (data flows both directions simultaneously, e.g., modern internet).
